# Keel R3 safety subsystem: hardware qualification

> **Note.** The DRV8411 `nFAULT` output (U1 pin 8) is not connected, so the
> fault-bus sources are the U11 supervisor, the U13 watchdog output and the two
> TPS2553-1 switches. RAM addresses are those of the release image
> (listed under "Diagnostic RAM words" in `../README.md`).

## Verified on hardware

Results of 2026-11-07, on an R3 board running this release image and the host
library libkeel 1.0:

- The motors arm and drive in both directions.
- The safety chain passes 17 of 17 tests: a stop drops ARMED within 9-37 us,
  heartbeat loss removes motor power through the MCU, and the re-arm hold-off
  and the emergency-stop latch work as specified.
- ARMED falls 51-71 ms after the host process is killed (design bound about
  86 ms).
- Host motor PWM over a 600 s soak under load: 99.9th-percentile lateness
  216 us, no overruns.

Not yet characterised: ultrasonic ranging (no sensor was fitted during the
tests), the servo path (J5 is not fitted on R3), long-term thermal behaviour,
ESD and EMC, and the detailed measurements in the table below.

## Characterisation tests

The entries below are the measurements that characterise the fitted board. They are not yet characterised beyond the functional results above. Software results and equivalent circuits narrow the questions but do not replace these measurements. Preserve the board/BOM/firmware hashes, instrument settings, source/load configuration and raw traces for each test; use the released component tolerances and declared accessory envelope when setting acceptance limits.

|Test|Required observation / acceptance purpose|
|---|---|
|Startup, slow ramps and reset|Record SYS5, SAFE3, common FAULT, NRST, both permits, actual enables and sensor enable. Motor and servo outputs must remain off through startup/reset/brownout and with raw EN held HIGH; sensor enable must stay off until two valid heartbeat falls and the first WDI falling edge after reset, then follow heartbeat health regardless of EN unless the strike limiter holds it off. Returning valid power must require a fresh zero-PWM ACK. Verify NRST_MODE = 3, RAM_PARITY_CHECK = 1 (SRAM parity checking disabled; otherwise the strike record is not carried across resets, and the firmware does not report that state), boot source, GPIO defaults and fitted MCU revision. Read `firmware_diagnostic` over SWD (0x20000064 in this image). First check for 0x48415244, the HardFault marker, which would otherwise decode as one strike. Otherwise bit 31 = sensor strike lockout, bits 16-17 = strikes, bits 0-10 = latched faults.|
|External watchdog and foreground stall|Force missing, stuck, too-fast and too-slow WDI/host heartbeat independently. Confirm the configured 0.8-1.2 ms closed window, 102.4-153.6 ms total frame and 22.5-27.5 ms WDO pulse against the exact fitted SKU. A stopped MCU foreground must lose actual enables and reset; repeated WDO pulses must never rearm a held EN. An absent Pi must permit stable all-off MCU operation. Measure the first WDI falling edge after reset release. It is estimated at roughly 80-85 ms: the image CRC at the 12 MHz reset clock takes roughly 29-33 ms, then the first kick comes 50 ms into the foreground loop. Confirm on the fitted SKU when the TPS3436 starts its frame after power-up and after its own WDO pulse (at WDO assertion or release). If that first kick can land later than the minimum 102.4 ms frame counted from that point, WDO resets can repeat and hold the controller off. That fails safe but stops operation, so demonstrate stable start-up after power-up, after a WDO reset and after a U30-caused reset.|
|Clock, ADC and internal watchdog|Measure loop and heartbeat timing across qualified temperature/supply; verify HSI bounds used for real-time lease calculations. Check VREFINT calibration/address and SAFE3 conversion against a calibrated meter; test acceptance edges at 3.10/3.50 V. Confirm ADC timeout, HardFault and IWDG paths cannot keep permissions asserted.|
|Interrupt observability and ACK aliasing|Inject PWM/EN/HB edges at different phases relative to SysTick, including 100 Hz soft-PWM motor inputs and the declared highest supported frequency. Measure interrupt latency and minimum captured pulse width. Activity throughout all ACK phases must prevent arming; no old permission may survive a recognized enable/fault edge. Scope raw and buffered inputs to exclude test-fixture artifacts.|
|Shortest hardware FAULT pulse|Exercise the actual fault-bus sources (the U11 supervisor, the U13 WDO output and both TPS2553-1 switches), including the feedback path where disabling a switch can clear its FAULT. Capture bus/NRST/permissions/PA12 simultaneously. Every recognized fault must force motor and servo permissions off and keep them off until a fresh ACK. Sensor enable (PA12) is forced off too but needs no ACK. After the resulting reset it returns once two valid heartbeat falls re-qualify and the first WDI kick has been driven. After an in-boot latch it returns once GPIO26 has been LOW with zero PWM and no servo request for 100 ms under a valid heartbeat. Repeated sensor-switch trips are bounded only by the strike limiter. It counts three events, each within 50 ms of a PA12 switch-on and without 2 s of healthy sensor power in between: an external pin reset, or a new F_EXTERNAL/F_RAIL latch without a reset. PA12 then stays off until a power-on reset or an invalid record. See the sensor-branch row. Record for each trip whether NRST actually asserted or only EXTI6 caught the edge. Selected 10 microsecond equivalent pulses reached reset; 1 microsecond reset cases are not characterised and must not be described as guaranteed. Verify actual source sink/leakage/capacitance against the model.|
|Partial power and wrong logic rail|With current-limited, declared test fixtures, cover Pi on/SAFE off, SAFE on/Pi off, normal rails, and Pi-side signals up to the declared 5.5 V input-buffer envelope. Measure all currents and phantom rails. Validate SAFE aggregate off-state injection <=200 microampere and <=0.1 V bleed allocation; host-status off voltage <=0.103 V is conditional on the modeled 10 microampere Ioff and pulldown. Validate actual HIGH margin under Pi pulls/loading.|
|Servo and sensor branch faults|Measure trip current, latch/recovery, turn-on/off and reverse current for the actual switches/resistors. Calculated current intervals are 444-568 mA servo and 108-154 mA sensor; these calculations do not prove thermal performance or ideal reverse blocking. Include connected/disconnected input source, external branch supply and approved accessory capacitance. The published 3-7 ms reverse turnoff leaves a finite backfeed interval to assess. Sensor power is available while disarmed, so a sustained sensor-branch short trips U30. Its FAULT either resets the MCU through the fault bus, or is caught by EXTI6 and drops PA12 before NRST acts (no reset; the core latches F_EXTERNAL). A switch-on sag of SAFE3 latches F_RAIL without a reset. With a healthy Pi heartbeat and the short applied, capture PA12, U30 FAULT, the fault bus, NRST, SAFE3 and SENSOR_5V, with the Pi (a) idle and disarmed and (b) re-arming repeatedly (a test program that calls `keel_request_arm` about once per second, and `keel_clear_fault` after each latch). Record which path each trip takes. Expect exactly three switch-on/trip cycles in any mix of reset, EXTI-only and sag trips; a short that develops after the sensor has been on for more than 50 ms gives one extra, uncounted trip first. Then PA12 must stay LOW across further pin and IWDG resets and in-boot re-qualification, while ACK, motors and servo still work, `firmware_diagnostic` bit 31 is set, and only a power-on reset (or an invalid record) restores it. Confirm that U30 asserts FAULT, and a sag reaches the ADC, well inside the 50 ms attribution window after EN rises, over temperature and approved load capacitance. A later trip is not counted and keeps retrying at the re-qualification or revalidation rate (every 151 ms in the built-image case with a FAULT 51 ms after each switch-on); this is a documented limit of this image. Also confirm that the fault-bus NRST pulse leaves PINRSTF as the only RCC_CSR2 reset flag on the fitted MCU revision, that the `.noinit` record survives that pulse with RAM_PARITY_CHECK = 1, and that a sensor short cannot pull SAFE3 into POR/BOR (PWRRSTF discards the record). Measure switch temperature over the three events, and confirm 2 s of healthy sensor power clears earlier strikes. **TPS3436 false-strike check:** the TPS3436 frame keeps running from the last WDI kick before a reset it did not cause itself. A WDO reset shortly after boot is therefore likely, and with fast heartbeats it could otherwise land within 50 ms of a sensor switch-on and count as a false strike. The firmware holds PA12 off until its first WDI kick of each boot, so such a WDO reset can only precede the switch-on. With 20 ms heartbeats, after U30-caused resets and after forced late first kicks, verify that no WDO reset is ever charged as a strike (bits 16-17 unchanged). The first kick is expected roughly 80-85 ms after reset release, so after a U30-caused reset it comes about 80-135 ms after the previous kick. A WDO reset before it is therefore the expected case, not an exception; record how often it occurs. After a WDO-caused reset the margin to the minimum 102.4 ms frame is small at best (see the watchdog row). The residual case is a WDO reset within 50 ms of a later switch-on. It needs kicks withheld for more than 50 ms just before that switch-on (fault bus LOW, loop or internal failure) or a foreground stall right after it, and is counted conservatively; characterise it.|
|Servo actuation envelope|Use each approved servo's startup, stall, travel and inertia. A healthy request must complete within the allowed burst/current budget; motors must be disabled during it. Verify lease/cooldown and loss-of-health paths, including repeated resets. Measure rail decay to the servo's minimum operating voltage and mechanical stop separately: enable LOW is not zero voltage or stopped motion. The illustrative 689 ms worst enable-off calculation assumes +/-6% MCU clock, an outstanding full external-watchdog frame and 3 ms switch-off; it does not include rail discharge.|
|Load budget and rail energy|Combine qualified motor, LED, expansion, sensor and servo states and test stalled/backdriven motors. Validate main eFuse coordination, source sag/overshoot, switch dissipation and upstream rail stress at tolerances. Current limits and firmware interlock must be checked against the actual motor/accessory and power-source envelope.|
|Host integration and service|Stress real Linux scheduling and the application's actual control loop; no separate blind timer may supply the heartbeat. Test missed deadlines, exceptions in the loop, process termination, GPIO20/21 reassignment, motor-line writes that bypass the host library, and unbounded sensor calls. Confirm ranging works while disarmed and that sensor power is removed when the heartbeat stops. With the sensor unpowered (heartbeat stopped, latched fault, strike lockout), confirm the host reports every measurement as unavailable and never as a clear path (libkeel reports `KEEL_RANGE_UNAVAILABLE`), including a measurement during which power is lost. Measure the application's real loop interval (the spacing of its `keel_cycle` calls) and jitter under load, including garbage-collection pauses. Log GPIO18 at the MCU pin (PB7) with a logic analyser. Every fall must come 30-70 ms after the previous one, except a heartbeat restart while disarmed (`KEEL_SNAP_HEARTBEAT_RESTARTED`). The host needs every loop gap to be at most 70 ms. If a call also comes less than 30 ms after a fall, a gap over 40 ms after it can miss the window (libkeel plans one stall of at most 40 ms, `KEEL_STALL_BUDGET_MS`). While arming, armed or in a servo burst, every miss must be reported as `KEEL_E_DEADLINE`: cause `TICK_LATE` for a loop gap over 70 ms and `HEARTBEAT_LATE` for a fall predicted more than 70 ms after the previous one, both before any pulse, or `HEARTBEAT_LATE` after the pulse when the 1 ms HIGH overran so that the fall lands more than 74 ms after the previous one. Check the MCU HSI clock against the +/-6% assumption behind the 30/70/74 ms limits and the 400 + 70 ms servo lease (MCU limit 500 ms). Interrupt arming in each acknowledgement phase (`snapshot.ack_phase`) with `keel_disarm`, `keel_emergency_stop` and a failed health report, and confirm that the next `keel_request_arm` arms (after `keel_clear_fault` and the 1 s re-arm hold-off where a fault latched). After an interruption in the second LOW, GPIO21 may rise about 100 ms into the next acknowledgement's first HIGH, with all four motor inputs LOW. The host must then lower GPIO26 and repeat the full sequence; libkeel does this once per arming request when it reads ARMED HIGH 90 ms or more into the first HIGH, and counts it in `snapshot.stale_ack_restarts`. Confirm that motor-line activity without the acknowledgement never moves the motors, that it latches F_PWM once the MCU is in its first ACK stage, and that a held brake (GPIO26 and all four inputs HIGH) keeps sensor power off until GPIO26 and all four inputs are LOW again. Scope all four DRV8411 inputs during servo preparation: the host's motor PWM must hold them LOW (coast) before the request, with GPIO26 HIGH (timing detail in the next row). Qualify the WDI programming fixture, connect-under-reset sequence, image verification and removal/tri-state of the fixture before normal use. Define debug/authenticity policy without irreversible automatic locking.|
|Soft-PWM zeroing before a servo request (oscilloscope)|Needs a board with J5 fitted and a servo-capable backend (on R3, libkeel reports no `KEEL_CAP_SERVO`). With the real Pi and the host's software PWM driving the motors, probe GPIO26, the four motor PWM pins at the Pi (BCM8/9/10/11) and at the MCU (PA0, PA1, PC14, PC15), SERVO_REQUEST (BCM19), MOTOR_EN_SAFE and SERVO_POWER_EN. Trigger on a marker written immediately before the coast command that precedes the servo request (`keel_set_motors(dev, 0, 0)` before `keel_servo_start`; a spare test GPIO, never GPIO17/LED_X). Start from driving at several duties, from brake (all four inputs at 100%) and from coast, with idle and loaded CPU. For each pin record when its coast takes effect and its last edge. Where soft-PWM channels apply their changes independently, also record every interval in which the two inputs of one DRV8411 bridge differ (a brief drive, e.g. brake followed by one input LOW) and the resulting motor current/motion. Acceptance: GPIO26 stays HIGH throughout; all four inputs are LOW and edge-free before SERVO_REQUEST rises (libkeel requires both motors coasting for at least 20 ms, `KEEL_SERVO_IDLE_MS`, before it raises the request; report the margin to the worst settle time); no F_PWM latch; any bridge-drive interval is characterised and accepted.|
|Assembly, EMC and environmental behavior|Measure fitted decoupling/return paths, supply integrity, temperature and protection response on the routed board. Run the project's relevant conducted/radiated/ESD tests with defined fixtures and criteria. Logical pad binding, zero DRC and lumped simulations cannot substitute for this.|

The bounded safety claim covers ordinary resets, missing/stuck/out-of-window heartbeat, foreground stalls, validly captured fault/activity edges and the qualified current/voltage/load envelope. Permanent stuck GPIOs, shorted power FETs, arbitrary code corruption, malicious replacement firmware, indistinguishable but dishonest host progress and external energy beyond that envelope are not proved safe by these tests. Two HIGH MCU permissions remain a hardware-reachable state; firmware mutual exclusion must not be described as a separate hardware interlock. Sensor-branch retries are bounded to three counted trips (a pin reset or an in-boot F_EXTERNAL/F_RAIL latch within 50 ms of a switch-on) without 2 s of healthy sensor power in between. A short that develops while the sensor has already been on for more than 50 ms adds one uncounted trip. A trip later than 50 ms after switch-on is never counted and is not bounded; this is a documented limit of this image. With RAM_PARITY_CHECK = 0 the image silently drops pin-reset strikes and the lockout at every reset, so resetting trips are then unbounded too. This is a known limitation of the image, and option-byte verification is its only guard.
