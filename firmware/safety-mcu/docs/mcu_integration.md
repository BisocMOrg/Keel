# Keel R3 safety controller: integration contract

> libkeel (`software/libkeel/`), the C library for the Pi, is the reference host implementation of this contract. The host requirements below apply to any host and name the libkeel call, status or constant that implements each; the libkeel specification (`software/libkeel/docs/spec.md`, sections 2.2, 9, 14 and 15) gives the host side in full.

**Release image. Arming, drive, the safety chain and host-loss shutdown are verified on hardware (2026-11-07); the detailed measurements in `qualification.md` are not yet characterised.** The circuit reference is the R3 schematic, `hardware/kicad/Keel.kicad_sch` (part U12; pin table under "MCU pin budget"). The policy core, strike limiter and timing constants are defined in `../src/safety_core.c` and `../src/safety_core.h`, and `../src/firmware.c` binds them to the U12 pins listed below. RAM addresses and sizes below are those of the release image `../release/safety_controller.hex` (SHA-256 `1608d866...`; the diagnostic RAM words are listed in `../README.md`). The DRV8411 `nFAULT` output (U1 pin 8) is not connected: the shared fault bus gates the driver's own `nSLEEP`, so the motor driver does not drive it.

## Hardware

U12, an STM32C011F4P6 (TSSOP-20), implements the acknowledgement logic. Its fixed firmware checks Pi heartbeat, acknowledgement, servo lease/cooldown, sensor power and feedback. Independent current switches, an external window watchdog and hardware gates remain outside Pi configuration control.

Two Nexperia 74LVC1G11GV,125 Schmitt/Ioff gates implement `MOTOR_EN_SAFE = MCU_MOTOR_PERMIT AND MOTOR_EN (GPIO26) AND MOTOR_FAULT` (U14) and the equivalent for `SERVO_POWER_EN` (U27). 1 kΩ series resistors (R28, R29) and 10 kΩ pulldowns (RN10, RN8) set the defaults of the MCU permission outputs. The four motor inputs run from the Pi straight to the DRV8411 (U1) with 10 kΩ pulldowns (RN9); `MOTOR_EN_SAFE` drives its `nSLEEP`. Mutual exclusion is a tested firmware invariant: two high MCU permissions can still produce two high enables in hardware. No independent mutual-exclusion circuit is claimed.

**The fault bus (`MOTOR_FAULT`, open drain, 22 kΩ pull-up R19) is driven only by the U11 supervisor, the U13 watchdog output and the U15/U30 switch `FAULT` outputs. It also resets the MCU** through 1 kΩ (R31) to NRST, which has a 47 kΩ pull-up (R30) and 1 nF C0G (C29). The MCU does not drive that bus; PA4 instead drives `MCU_READY`. Reset makes every MCU output high-impedance/pulled low. Motor and servo permissions then require a fresh acknowledgement; sensor power (PA12) needs none and returns after two valid heartbeat falls and the firmware's first WDI kick unless the strike limiter holds it off (see Firmware). Because the bus also drives NRST, a finite watchdog pulse cannot leave a frozen HIGH permission to be re-enabled when the pulse ends.

U13, a TPS3436CABFDDDFRQ1 (pinout C), is fixed at ratio 128: 0.8–1.2 ms closed window, 102.4–153.6 ms total frame and 22.5–27.5 ms WDO LOW. The 1 ms option is ±20 %, not ±10 %. Configuration pins are hardwired (SET0 to GND, SET1 to `SAFE_3V3`); MR is tied to `SAFE_3V3`, not to the reset/fault bus, and WD_EN is `PROG_N`, so SW5 pole 4 disables the watchdog for programming. Foreground firmware produces falling edges nominally every 50 ms; no ISR/DMA feeds it.

**U16, a TPS70933DBVT, supplies the independent `SAFE_3V3` from `SYS_5V`** (pins 1 IN, 2 GND, 3 EN, 4 NC, 5 OUT). **EN floats, as TI permits; never tie it to VIN**, because EN is 6.5 V-limited while IN supports 30 V. Use 4.7 µF/50 V capacitors with ≥2.2 µF effective output and ESR ≤0.2 Ω. U11, U13, U14, U27, U29 and the fault-bus pull-up R19 run from `SAFE_3V3`. A 470 Ω bleed (R25) bounds a declared 200 µA aggregate off-state injection to ≤0.096 V, at about 23 mW normal dissipation. This allocation is not yet characterised on an assembled board.

U29, an SN74LVC541APWR powered from `SAFE_3V3`, buffers the seven Pi control/PWM inputs plus the fault bus for the MCU. Bare STM32 pins are not assumed 5.5 V-tolerant when their supply is zero. The Pi-side inputs have pulldowns: 10 kΩ on the four motor inputs (RN9), the heartbeat (R47) and the servo request (RN6), and 22 kΩ on GPIO26 (R45). No external Pi 3.3 V ADC divider is fitted; the ADC monitors internal VREFINT/SAFE3 only.

U15, a TPS2553DBVR-1 (active-HIGH enable, latch-off), supplies `SERVO_5V` with a 52.3 kΩ ILIM (R27): calculated 444–568 mA, 502 mA nominal. J5 pin 2 and its RN3 pull-up element use `SERVO_5V`, so the signal pull-up cannot power a disabled accessory from `SYS_5V`. The identical sensor switch U30 uses a 210 kΩ ILIM (R32): 108–154 mA; J1 pin 1 and its RN3 element use `SENSOR_5V`. PA12 drives `SENSOR_POWER_EN` with a 10 kΩ pulldown (RN10). Both switches have finite reverse turn-off (3–7 ms), not ideal isolation; real regeneration and source faults are not yet characterised.

U28, a 74LVC2G17GV,125 powered from the Pi's 3.3 V, conveys `MCU_READY` to GPIO20 and the actual `MOTOR_EN_SAFE` to GPIO21, through 1 kΩ (R20, R23) with 10 kΩ host-side pulldowns (RN6). Array/TCR/leakage fixtures give HIGH ≥2.128 V against the BCM2711's 2.0 V criterion and off-node ≤0.103 V for 10 µA Ioff. These are leakage bounds, not zero-backpower claims. HIGH remains healthy/ready on GPIO20 and motor-armed on GPIO21. GPIO21 is LOW during servo operation. BCM18 (physical 12) is the heartbeat; BCM19 (physical 35) is the servo request. Physical 11 (BCM17) remains LED_X.

EXTI routing: lines 0, 1 and 3 to port A (PA0, PA1, PA3), lines 6 and 7 to port B (PB6, PB7), lines 14 and 15 to port C. All five outputs are push-pull on GPIOA, so one BSRR write still sets or clears them together. No internal pulls are enabled. No timer channel, ADC pin or other alternate function is used: WDI is a GPIO driven by the foreground loop, and the ADC reads only the internal VREFINT channel.

## Firmware

Boot order in `main()`: configure GPIO/EXTI with every output driven LOW; read the RCC_CSR2 reset flags; read the FLASH_OPTR RAM_PARITY_CHECK bit; restore and store the sensor strike record; clear the flags (RMVF); start SysTick and the IWDG. Only then does it check the IWDG register update, NRST_MODE = 3, the image CRC and the ADC calibration/ready sequence, before it enters the foreground state machine. The CRC and NRST checks therefore run after RMVF and after the IWDG has started. If any of them fails, `internal_ok` is false: every permission, READY and PA12 stay LOW and neither watchdog is serviced. The TPS3436 then normally resets the MCU first: its WDO pulse (at most 153.6 ms after the last kick) pulls the fault bus and NRST LOW, which the MCU records as a pin reset (PINRSTF only). Only if NRST cannot act does the IWDG reset it, after about 0.26 s (IWDGRSTF). Either way the strike record is kept and no strike is added, because PA12 never came on in that boot. Start-up timing: the MCU runs at the 12 MHz reset clock, and the image CRC dominates start-up. The foreground loop is estimated to start roughly 29–33 ms after reset release, and its first WDI kick follows 50 ms later, roughly 80–85 ms after reset release. These are estimates, not yet characterised on hardware (see `qualification.md`). Reset defaults all power/permission/ready outputs low. ES0569 Rev4 section2.2.5's dummy SRAM read is inserted before any stack/data access. Stop mode, DMA, I2C, USART and timer output peripherals are unused; ADC conversions are separate single shots. Boards are assembled with U12 blank.

Pi falling heartbeat intervals must be 20–80 ms. The host places them 30–70 ms apart and emits each one from its real control loop, after that loop's health checks (see sensor power below). libkeel emits a fall only from `keel_cycle` or `keel_tick`; a tick that reports a health value other than `KEEL_HEALTH_ALL` emits none and stops the heartbeat (`KEEL_E_HEALTH`). Blind PWM/DMA or a separate healthy thread can conceal a failed application; this protocol does not prove application health independently of its implementation. External WDI service requires a healthy foreground pass and either fresh Pi heartbeat or positively verified all-off permissions/feedback. Thus disarmed boot/fault recovery can run without an already-booted Pi, while enabled actuation requires fresh Pi health. ISRs never service watchdogs.

Acknowledgement is GPIO26 LOW for at least 100 ms, then HIGH, LOW and HIGH for at least 100 ms each (no maximum), with zero PWM and servo request LOW throughout. The host holds every level 150 ms, which covers a 6% slow MCU clock, and starts the first HIGH only once READY is HIGH. libkeel's `keel_request_arm` runs this sequence over the following ticks, times each level from the completion of its write (`KEEL_ACK_LEVEL_MS`) and fails with `KEEL_E_ACK_TIMEOUT` if arming has not completed within 2 s. Only final qualification arms. PWM/EN/heartbeat EXTI events prevent sub-poll aliasing; a held enable through reset/fault never rearms. Atomic BSRR writes select permissions, and actual gate outputs are checked. EXTI6 (PB6) latches recognized short faults and clears outputs. Device pin specifications (EXTI ≥20 ns; NRST ≥350 ns) do not replace board/source pulse measurements.

Servo needs a fresh request after acknowledgement, all four motor PWM inputs LOW, a 500 ms lease and a 2 s cooldown, including after reset. A brake (all four inputs HIGH) counts as activity just as driving does, so the host brings all four inputs LOW (coast) and keeps GPIO26 HIGH before it raises the request. libkeel's `keel_servo_start` requires both motors coasting for at least 20 ms (`KEEL_SERVO_IDLE_MS`) and its own 2.2 s cooldown elapsed (`KEEL_SERVO_COOLDOWN_MS`), limits the lease to 400 ms (`KEEL_SERVO_LEASE_MAX_MS`) and ends it no later than the first tick at or after the lease end. With ticks at most 70 ms apart the request is LOW within 470 ms, under the MCU's 500 ms even on a 6% fast clock. A request LOW ends a normal burst and disarms; timeout latches a fault. Serial requests cannot extend a lease or change current limits. Actual servo startup, stall and mechanics are not yet characterised (J5 is not fitted on R3, where libkeel reports no `KEEL_CAP_SERVO`). The servo connector (J5) is for timed servo bursts; continuous power for addressable LEDs on it is not supported and has no software bypass.

**Sensor power (PA12) = fresh valid heartbeat AND fault bus HIGH with no pending fault edge AND SAFE3 in range AND internal/loop checks AND no latched fault AND no strike lockout AND at least one WDI kick already driven in this boot.** It is independent of GPIO26 and arming, so ultrasonic readings work while disarmed; motor/servo permits, their exclusion, lease/cooldown, READY and ACK are unchanged (the lockout input reaches only the sensor output). It is off from reset until two valid heartbeat falls and the first WDI falling edge of that boot (about 50 ms after the foreground loop starts, roughly 80–85 ms after reset release; see the strike limiter). It is also off whenever the last fall is more than 80 ms old, on a recognized fault edge and while latched. After a latch it returns only when GPIO26 has been LOW with zero PWM and no servo request for 100 ms under a valid heartbeat (the start of a fresh ACK). A Pi idling disarmed therefore re-enables it about 100 ms after every latch. Because sensor power follows the heartbeat and not GPIO26, a host that keeps its falls inside the window keeps the sensor powered through the ACK, armed driving, servo bursts and the following disarm. **Host heartbeat schedule.** The MCU compares fall intervals with 20–80 ms on its own clock, declared within ±6% and sampled every 1 ms. The host schedules falls from a monotonic clock, not per loop call: the first loop pass at least 30 ms after the previous fall drives a 1 ms GPIO18 HIGH, so no two falls are closer than 30 ms, and a loop that always reaches the next pass in time keeps them 30–70 ms apart. That is inside the MCU's 20–80 ms with 10 ms margin for the clock tolerance and the 1 ms sampling. Only a loop pass starts a pulse, so arming requests, refusals and non-latching stops never bring a fall forward; the first fall comes at least 30 ms after open, and after `keel_clear_fault` at least 30 ms after the last fall before the latch (libkeel: `KEEL_HEARTBEAT_MIN_MS`, `KEEL_HEARTBEAT_PULSE_US`, `KEEL_TICK_WINDOW_MS`). Jitter stays inside the window when every gap is at most 40 ms. Gaps of up to 70 ms are safe only when no pass comes less than 30 ms after a fall: such a pass emits no pulse, and a gap over 40 ms after it can put the next fall more than 70 ms after the previous one although no gap exceeds 70 ms. libkeel therefore plans one loop stall of at most 40 ms (`KEEL_STALL_BUDGET_MS`). While arming, armed or in a servo burst the host must detect a miss before it emits a fall outside the MCU's window. libkeel fails before pulsing on a loop gap over 70 ms (`KEEL_E_DEADLINE`, cause `TICK_LATE`) and on a fall predicted more than 70 ms after the previous one (`HEARTBEAT_LATE`), and after the pulse with `HEARTBEAT_LATE` when an overrunning 1 ms HIGH puts the fall more than 74 ms after the previous one (74 ms reads under 80 ms even on a 6% fast MCU clock). Each of these latches FAULTED and stops the heartbeat, so the MCU latches F_HEARTBEAT and removes sensor power; after `keel_clear_fault` the next tick restarts the heartbeat and the sensor returns about 0.1–0.2 s later. Every other fault latch, and `keel_emergency_stop`, stops the heartbeat in the same way, while `keel_disarm` keeps it running, and with it sensor power. While disarmed a late loop pass restarts the heartbeat instead (`KEEL_SNAP_HEARTBEAT_RESTARTED`): the MCU latches F_HEARTBEAT and re-qualifies after two valid falls and 100 ms of quiet, a sensor-power gap of about 0.2 s. Loop periods of 71–100 ms can never arm: the MCU grants no motor permit, and libkeel fails the second tick after `keel_request_arm` with `TICK_LATE`. With on-time ticks the acknowledgement completes about 0.6–0.85 s after `keel_request_arm`. An ACK interrupted after its first HIGH can leave the MCU in S_WAIT_H2, which has no timeout while the heartbeat stays valid. The next ACK's first HIGH then completes that sequence, and the MCU grants the motor permit about 100 ms into it, with all motor inputs LOW. The host must recognise this and lower GPIO26: S_ARMED goes to S_DISARMED without a fault. It then repeats the full sequence. libkeel does this when it reads ARMED HIGH 90 ms or more into the first HIGH, once per `keel_request_arm` (`snapshot.stale_ack_restarts`); ARMED earlier in the first HIGH, or a second stale completion, fails with `KEEL_E_FEEDBACK` (`ACK_FEEDBACK`). Cutting S_HIGH1 or S_HIGH2 short, before 100 ms, latches F_ACK (sensor off for about 100 ms). **Motor-line activity without the acknowledgement cannot use the motors:** it produces no ACK. While a host heartbeat runs disarmed, 100 ms of GPIO26 LOW, zero PWM and no servo request puts the MCU in the first ACK stage (READY HIGH). Any motor-input level or edge then latches F_PWM: READY LOW, sensor off, no motor permit. A brake (GPIO26 and all four inputs HIGH) holds that latch, so the sensor stays off until GPIO26 and all four inputs are LOW again and 100 ms of quiet have passed. A host therefore drives the motor inputs only after a completed ACK; libkeel's backend refuses a drive or brake unless ENABLE is HIGH, the servo request is LOW and a fresh read shows ARMED HIGH (libkeel SPEC section 17.5). The sensor has no feedback input; WDI service and motor/servo feedback checks do not use it. The TPS2553 108–154 mA limit and its fault-bus connection remain.

**Sensor strike limiter.** U30 (TPS2553-1, latch-off) FAULT is on the common fault bus. A trip either resets the MCU through NRST, or EXTI6 catches the FAULT edge and drops PA12 within microseconds, before NRST acts. In the second case U30 unlatches, the MCU keeps running, and the core only latches F_EXTERNAL. A switch-on sag of SAFE3 latches F_RAIL the same way. Without a limiter a shorted branch would be re-enabled indefinitely: about ten times per second by heartbeat revalidation after each reset, roughly every 110 ms by the 100 ms quiet re-qualification while the Pi idles disarmed (about 560 trips a minute in a no-reset probe), or at every fresh ACK start. Two copies of a 20-byte record live in a `.noinit` SRAM section that startup neither zeroes nor copies. The record holds a magic word, the ms since PA12 last rose while that switch-on is not yet charged with a strike, the strike count, the PA12 state and an inverted rotate-xor check word. Every 1 ms pass follows one order. It reads the lockout from the working record, runs the core, and forms PA12 as the core permit AND no lockout AND at least one WDI kick already driven in this boot. It then updates the record with that PA12 value and with `sensor_guard_trip()` (the core newly latched with F_EXTERNAL or F_RAIL in this pass). It stores slot 0 and then slot 1, and only then writes the output register. A reset that tears one store therefore leaves the other valid, and a reset caused by a switch-on always finds that switch-on in the record. A strike is counted once per switch-on of PA12, in one of two ways. In the running boot: the core newly latches with F_EXTERNAL or F_RAIL on a pass at most 50 ms after the pass that switched PA12 on. At the next boot: an external pin reset whose record shows PA12 rose at most 50 ms earlier. A counted switch-on is marked as charged, so a pin reset that follows the same in-boot latch cannot count it again. A second NRST during a record store cannot count it twice either. If it lands before slot 0 is complete, slot 0 is either unchanged or fails its check, so the next boot uses the record from before the strike and derives the same single strike; once slot 0 is complete, the new slot 0 wins. Boot and main-loop cases cover a reset after every word of the boot-time and in-boot stores. An in-boot latch counts only if it includes F_EXTERNAL or F_RAIL; heartbeat, PWM, ACK and similar latches never count (main-loop case). The lockout is re-evaluated on every pass, so it takes effect inside the running boot. At boot the firmware reads RCC_CSR2 (bit names as in the retained ST `stm32c011xx.h`). PWRRSTF (POR/PDR/BOR) means power-on: the record is not read and strikes start at 0. PINRSTF with no other flag means an external pin reset. Any other flag (IWDGRSTF, WWDGRSTF, SFTRSTF, OBLRSTF, LPWRRSTF) keeps the record without a strike, because internal resets also pulse NRST and may set PINRSTF. If the FLASH_OPTR RAM_PARITY_CHECK bit reads 0 (SRAM parity checking enabled), a slot never written since power-up could read as a parity error. The record is then not read and the boot is treated as a power-on. Option-byte verification must therefore confirm RAM_PARITY_CHECK = 1 (parity checking disabled). With parity enabled, only in-boot strikes count; pin-reset strikes and the lockout are forgotten at every reset, so a short that resets the MCU is retried without limit. Nothing reports that state: no diagnostic bit shows it, and the controller otherwise runs normally, whereas a wrong NRST_MODE stops the controller. The option-byte check is the only guard. Flagging the state or holding PA12 off would change the image, so this is a known limitation of this image. The record is stored before RMVF clears the flags, so a reset in between still reports its real cause. 2 s of continuous sensor power clears strikes below the limit. At three strikes the core's `sensor_strike_lockout` input holds PA12 off (firmware masks PA12 again). Over SWD, `firmware_diagnostic` (0x20000064 in this image) shows bit 31 = lockout and bits 16-17 = strikes; bits 0-10 remain the latched fault set. Compare it with 0x48415244 first: that HardFault marker would otherwise decode as strikes = 1, lockout = 0. The record slots are at 0x20000084 (slot 0) and 0x20000098 (slot 1), each holding magic, rise age, strikes, PA12 state and check word. These addresses belong to this image; another image may move them. Resulting bound: with a short already present at switch-on, three switch-on/trip cycles, then PA12 stays off. That holds whether each trip resets the MCU, is caught by EXTI6, or sags SAFE3, and in any mix. A short that develops while the sensor has already been on for more than 50 ms gives one uncounted trip first, so four trips in total (unit tests S11 and S18). The count restarts only after 2 s of healthy sensor power, a power-on reset or an invalid record. PA12 also waits for the first WDI kick of each boot. The TPS3436 frame keeps running from the last kick before a reset that it did not cause itself, such as a U30 trip. The new boot's first kick comes about 50 ms after the foreground loop starts, roughly 80–85 ms after reset release (the image CRC runs first). That is about 80–135 ms after the last kick before the reset, so against the 102.4–153.6 ms frame a WDO pin reset before that first kick is the normal case after such a reset, not an edge case. Without the gate the sensor could switch on after two heartbeat falls (20–40 ms after the loop starts with 20 ms heartbeats), so that WDO reset would fall inside the strike window and count as a false strike. With the gate, such a WDO reset can only occur before PA12 has risen in that boot, so it cannot strike. After a WDO-caused reset, whether the first kick meets the frame depends on where the TPS3436 restarts its frame after its own WDO pulse. If it restarts at WDO assertion, the kick comes about 103–113 ms into the frame and WDO resets could repeat, holding the controller off. This is not yet characterised. A watchdog timeout after the first kick needs more than 102.4 ms without kicks. The firmware kicks every 50 ms whenever the sensor may be on, so a WDO reset inside the window of a later switch-on requires kicks to have been withheld (fault bus LOW, loop/internal failure) for over 50 ms just before that switch-on, or a foreground stall right after it. Both are genuine faults and are counted conservatively. The main-loop boot test `loop_fast_heartbeat_PA12_waits_for_first_wdi_kick` shows the gate.

**Recovery: a power-on reset, or a boot that finds no valid record; a fresh ACK does not clear the lockout.** The record is invalid, and the next boot starts with no strikes, whenever both slots fail their check. That happens when SRAM is overwritten by an SWD programming or debug session (flash loaders run from SRAM), when a new image places `.noinit` elsewhere or changes the record format, or when the RAM_PARITY_CHECK option is enabled so the record is not read. Reasons for this recovery policy: the bound then holds whatever the Pi software does (a host that re-arms in a loop would otherwise restart the trips at its ACK rate); the motor/servo state machine gains no new observer; and a shorted harness needs physical rework, which normally involves powering down. The lockout is not visible on GPIO20/21, because READY and ARMED are deliberately unchanged; SWD can read `firmware_diagnostic` and the record. With sensor power off (heartbeat stopped, latched fault or strike lockout) the HC-SR04 never raises ECHO, so a host ranging routine that accepts a timed-out echo wait as a reading reports a false clear path. The host must report such a measurement as unavailable, never as "no obstacle". libkeel does this: `keel_range_start` and `keel_range_measure` never drive TRIG unless the heartbeat is qualified and a fresh READY read is HIGH, and report `KEEL_RANGE_UNAVAILABLE` instead; a measurement whose ECHO does not rise within 5 ms of the trigger is `KEEL_RANGE_UNAVAILABLE` too. Limits (not yet characterised on hardware): a trip later than 50 ms after switch-on is never counted and keeps retrying at the re-qualification or revalidation rate (every 151 ms in the built-image case with a FAULT 51 ms after each switch-on). This is a documented limit of this image. An unrelated pin reset, or F_EXTERNAL/F_RAIL latch (motor driver, servo switch, WDO, rail noise), within 50 ms of a switch-on also counts as a strike. An intermittent fault that allows 2 s of healthy sensor power between trips is never locked out. Power-on recovery assumes a sensor short cannot brown out SAFE3 (PWRRSTF discards the record).

## Evidence and remaining limits

Compiled ARM policy tests: **2,832,503 assertions pass**, including directed cases and two 100,000-step deterministic adversarial runs; both compare sensor power with a reference predicate at every step (check 184 in `../tests/unit/test_safety_core.c`). Strike-limiter tests cover random power-on SRAM (200,000 records, none accepted), every single-bit corruption, the 50 ms window edge for pin-reset and in-boot strikes, reset-cause handling, torn slot stores, the 2 s clear, one strike per switch-on (charging), lockout persistence and power-on recovery. They also check `sensor_guard_trip()` against an input predicate. A device model mirrors the firmware main loop, with sensor shorts that reset the MCU, U30 FAULTs caught by EXTI6 without a reset, SAFE3 sags, and a Pi that re-ACKs every second. A no-reset probe (FAULT 7 ms after each switch-on, Pi idle and disarmed) gives three trips in 60 s instead of about 560 without the limiter. In the core and device-model runs, a lock-step reference core without the lockout gives identical motor/servo/READY/WDI outputs and state at every step. These are not 2,832,503 independent hardware tests. Built-image cases (`../tests/emulator/test_firmware_boot.py`): 21 boot-path and 11 main-loop cases pass, including three pin-map cases. The boot-path cases cover RCC_CSR2 decoding, the parity-option guard, no record read after PWRRSTF or with parity checking enabled, both slots stored before RMVF, and a second NRST after every word of the boot's record store (still one strike). The boot-path case `gpio_exti_configuration_matches_u12_pin_map` starts the GPIO/EXTI registers from their reset values and checks, at the first main-loop WFI, every U12 pin's mode, output type and pull, each edge input alone on its EXTI line with EXTICR routed to its port and the right edges, no other line unmasked, the three EXTI IRQs enabled, SWD/GPIOF untouched and all outputs LOW. In the main loop they show:
- both slots rewritten on every pass before the single output write, and every PA12 rise recorded first;
- the EXTI6 handler itself driving PA12, READY, motor and servo LOW on every FAULT edge;
- a locked record never setting PA12, while READY, motor arming after an ACK and a servo burst are unaffected;
- PA12 after two heartbeat falls and the first WDI kick;
- three in-boot strikes (EXTI-caught FAULT or SAFE3 sag), then a lockout that survives a pin reset and clears at power-on;
- a FAULT edge between input sampling and the output write (injected at the `safety_step` call) keeping PA12 LOW although the core still permits it that pass, then one strike;
- F_HEARTBEAT and F_PWM latches 7 ms after a switch-on adding no strike;
- a FAULT 51 ms after each switch-on, retried every 151 ms and never counted (the documented limit);
- one strike when an in-boot latch is followed by a pin reset, also when that reset tears the strike pass's record store at any word.
- (pin map) from the first ACK stage, a GPIO26 double edge on PA3/EXTI3 latching F_ACK, a PWM edge on each of EXTI0/1/14/15 and a PWM level on each of PA0/PA1/PC14/PC15 latching F_PWM, and PB6 held LOW latching F_EXTERNAL, each dropping READY, with nothing latched without a stimulus;
- (pin map) a fault-bus fall inside `apply()` after its interrupts are masked (EXTI6 not yet serviced): the PB6 level read alone makes that pass's output write drop PA12, READY, motor and servo, and the pending edge then latches F_EXTERNAL.

Startup cases (`../tests/emulator/test_startup.py`): the IWDG initial reload and runtime lease at LSI 29.5, 32 and 34 kHz; a stuck IWDG status and a missing ADC CCRDY, each failing closed with every output LOW; invalid option bytes and a wrong image CRC, each reported at its startup stage; and an image built from `firmware.c` with a deliberately broken CHSELR write before every ADC start, which the model shows starting conversions before CCRDY while every output stays LOW. Host contract: libkeel's simulator steps a hash-guarded copy of `safety_core.c`/`.h` every simulated millisecond, and its reference-model suite (`software/libkeel/tests/core/test_reference.c` and `test_servo_range.c`) runs the library against it. Every tick period from 1 to 70 ms arms with exactly two GPIO26 rises, no MCU fault and falls 30–70 ms apart. Periods of 71–100 ms never obtain a motor permit and fail with `TICK_LATE`. An early tick followed by a long gap fails with `HEARTBEAT_LATE` before any late fall. An acknowledgement interrupted in its second LOW is completed by the stale-completion restart without an MCU fault. A late tick while disarmed restarts the heartbeat, and the MCU re-qualifies it. A brake while armed never trips the MCU. Servo leases of 20, 150 and 400 ms at tick periods of 1, 5, 10, 25, 50 and 70 ms never trip F_COOLDOWN, F_PWM or F_SERVO_TIMEOUT and never grant both permits. A seeded random walk of public calls checks every simulated millisecond that no motor input is HIGH while the MCU does not permit the motors, that the servo request rises only with idle motors, that both permits are never granted together and that the host causes no MCU fault; after every call it checks that heartbeat falls come only from ticks, 20–80 ms apart, unless the heartbeat was stopped or restarted on purpose. Peripheral initialization/interrupt latency still needs board bring-up. Image size is 4,360 B of 16 KiB flash. Static RAM is 172 bytes (128 B BSS, 40 B `.noinit` strike record, 4 B initialized data), plus stack.

Enable-off bound for an MCU hang (calculation): with the ±6% clock bound, the 500 ms software deadline becomes ≤532 ms; adding a full external-watchdog frame and the 3 ms switch-off gives an illustrative bound of 689 ms. Rail discharge and mechanical stopping add separate load-dependent delay.

These tests cover missing/stuck/early/late heartbeat, foreground stalls, ordinary MCU reset, recognized external faults, malformed acknowledgement and held/repeated servo requests. They do not prove behaviour under permanent semiconductor output shorts, stuck power FETs, arbitrary firmware corruption, arbitrary glitches, real stall heating, actual rail ramps or EMC. The R3 schematic and BOM are the reference for the fitted parts.

The bottom pads TP39–TP43 (`SAFE_3V3`, GND, NRST, SWDIO, SWCLK) and TP44 (WDI) give an external-probe path. Normal programming uses SW5 from the Pi (see `../README.md`); pole 4 disables the U13 watchdog and clamps both MCU permissions LOW (D12). With an external probe, keep the Pi and actuators disconnected and the permissions held off; a fixture may feed WDI during connect-under-reset programming and must tri-state it before the firmware runs. Verify image CRC, main-flash boot, NRST_MODE = 3 and RAM_PARITY_CHECK = 1 (SRAM parity checking disabled; with 0 the strike record is not carried across resets). Do not disable the watchdogs in firmware for debugging. These scripts do not change OTP/readout-protection settings; released boards keep readout protection at level 0 with no debug lock (the option-byte check requires RDP 0xAA).
